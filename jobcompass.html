AI-Powered Career Platform for UK Tech Job Seekers

September 2025 – Present · Personal product · Glasgow

JobCompass is the career product I am building for UK tech professionals and graduates, including international candidates who need a visa sponsor. It runs one loop: Resume → Discover → Match → Apply → Prepare → Track. Roles come from company career sites rather than LinkedIn or Indeed, and each listing is tagged with visa sponsorship intelligence from the UK Home Office register of licensed sponsors.

View on GitHub Architecture blog

Problem

A UK tech search is split across hundreds of career sites. Sponsorship status is hard to see, so international graduates waste applications on employers who cannot hire them. ATS filters reject weak resumes with no feedback. Applying one form at a time does not improve the response rate, and interview prep starts too late. JobCompass keeps those steps in one place, and it keeps automation behind human gates instead of an unsupervised apply-bot.

What it does

  • Resume engine. A CV upload seeds the profile. Skills are grouped, experience bullets are checked for a measurable impact, and a re-upload shows a structured diff. Claude drafts an editable summary, and an ATS score covers parsability, keywords, content, formatting, and completeness.
  • Direct discovery. A Playwright agent collects roles from company career websites, with per-domain rate limits, circuit breakers, change detection, and deduplication. Salary is read from the posting when it is listed, and estimated with a confidence level when it is not.
  • Three boards. Graduate schemes, sponsored roles ranked by recent sponsorship activity, and non-sponsored roles for UK citizens and settled-status holders. Filters cover recency, distance, salary, and visa route.
  • Match scoring. Semantic similarity, keyword overlap, and seniority fit are materialised and refreshed when the resume or a listing changes, so job tables stay fast.
  • AutoApplier. Reads ATS forms at runtime. It recognises Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, and iCIMS, and falls back to generic field detection for other layouts. It pauses for CAPTCHA, low-confidence fields, salary, notice period, and right to work. A match-score gate (default 65%) and a daily cap limit volume.
  • Tracker and prep. Applications move through a status lifecycle. Gmail can update that status from recruiter mail, follow-up drafts wait for approval, and interview prep falls back to a template if the model is unavailable.

Architecture

The rule is simple: a module that talks to the open web is an agent. A module that works on stored product data is a deterministic pipeline. Scraping, AutoApplier, and company research adapt when sites change. Auth, resume processing, match materialisation, the tracker, and analytics stay in FastAPI services with PostgreSQL as the source of truth.

  • Agents: Playwright scraper, human-in-the-loop AutoApplier, and a research agent that enriches sponsorship history and salary signals.
  • Pipelines: Auth.js sessions (Google or GitHub OAuth, or email and password) with short-lived JWTs in httpOnly cookies, the four-phase resume engine, materialised match scores in PostgreSQL with pgvector, the application state machine, and analytics.
  • Sponsorship data: a weekly parse of the public Home Office register of licensed sponsors, joined onto each job as a deterministic pipeline. The sponsored board can be ranked by recent sponsorship activity.
  • Privacy: resumes live in Cloudflare R2 and profile data in PostgreSQL. The API can export or delete a user’s data. Auth stays on standard OAuth and email login, away from identity checks that would pull GDPR special-category data.
